# Wake mode for multi-agent orchestrators

Interactive CLI and app-server MultiAgentV2 roots now sleep until a subagent reports back instead of polling `wait_agent` by default. Set `agent_polling = "enabled"` to keep polling.

## Summary

With `agent_polling = "disabled"` (the default), an interactive MultiAgentV2
root no longer calls `wait_agent` to check on its children. A child's
completion starts a turn on the root directly, so it sleeps between turns
rather than polling on a timer. Exec sessions and child sessions continue to
poll in this stage. Esc pauses wakeups and holds child results until the user's
next message; held results are never lost or delivered twice.

## Problem, with measured numbers

These are measurements from the author's own sessions, not general benchmarks.
The five-session scan classified `nenpi` rollouts with an ad-hoc per-turn
classifier; its totals include each session's subagents and count cumulative
input+cached tokens. See [issue #19](https://github.com/epsalmond/codex/issues/19)
for the scan command, classification method, and token breakdown.

- One gpt-6-astra orchestrator session made 93 `wait_agent` calls, and 47 of
  them (50.5%) timed out. Turns following `wait_agent` used 10.59M of the
  parent's 31.39M tokens (33.7%), 99.5% of it cached input.
- Across five diverse high-token sessions in 21 days (1,204M cumulative
  input+cached tokens, including subagents), 330M (27.4%) were attributed to
  waiting turns. `wait_agent` polling alone accounted for 202.5M (16.8%) over 766
  calls, typically with `timeout_ms: 10000`.

## What changed

- The root wakes on child completion instead of polling `wait_agent`.
- `wait_agent` is removed only for the root in wake mode; subagents keep it.
- Esc pauses wakeups; a later user message resumes them.
- New experimental app-server notification `thread/wakeups/updated`, with
  fields `threadId`, `paused`, and `queuedResults`.
- The TUI shows a queued-results line while wakeups are paused.
- Model-visible text changes: the root is told reports arrive on their own;
  subagents are told, "If you are blocked, end your turn with your question;
  your parent will reply with a follow-up."
- Child results are kept across `/compact`, `/review`, `!cmd` replacement,
  approval aborts, and blocked prompts.

## Polling configuration

```toml
[features.multi_agent_v2]
agent_polling = "enabled"
```

or for a single run, `codex -c 'features.multi_agent_v2.agent_polling="enabled"'`.

## Compatibility

MultiAgentV2 is enabled by default, and `agent_polling` defaults to
`"disabled"` for interactive roots. Set it to `"enabled"` to preserve polling.
Exec sessions and child sessions continue to use `wait_agent` in this stage.
Older builds do not recognize the `agent_polling` string setting.
If a local profile still has `wait_agent_enabled` from an unreleased development
build, remove that entry before upgrading; this build accepts `agent_polling` only.

## Known limits and later stages

- Nested orchestrators (a subagent with its own children) don't wake yet
  (stage 3).
- `codex exec` stays in `wait_agent` polling because the process exits when its root turn ends, even if children are still running. Keeping exec alive for wakeups is stage 2.
- Background processes and cells waking on exit (stage 4).
- Every root interrupt, including the app-server's own permission-error
  interrupt, pauses wakeups.
